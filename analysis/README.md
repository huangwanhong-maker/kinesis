# Analysis

Status: **draft** (2026-11-13). Module `kinesis::analysis`.

## 1. Concepts

What a system does over a long time is asked at the level at which the system is known. The invariants of a flow or a map (its exponents, its equilibria, its sections, its attractors as a parameter moves, its basins) need the system as a presentation: the field and, for the exponents and the equilibria, its Jacobian, which the terms give exactly. The signs of criticality (slowing down before a transition, correlations without a scale, avalanches) work on a record alone; each is an indicator with known ways of misleading, which are stated, and none is a certificate of a transition or of a critical state.

A landscape is a function on the state space whose valleys are where a system stays. The word covers several functions, which agree only in special cases; each one here says which it is: the potential of a gradient system, or the stationary landscape −ln p_ss of a stochastic system. Lifted to operators, a system acts linearly on densities whatever it is, and Ulam's method makes that lift a stochastic matrix on a partition of the state space. Theory: [docs/theory.md](../docs/theory.md).

## 2. Content

- `invariants.hpp`: `Exponents`, `lyapunov(d, x0, o)` (tangent vectors carried by the exact Jacobian and orthonormalised as in physis; Kaplan and Yorke's dimension), `block_errors`; `Equilibrium`, `Box`, `newton`, `equilibrium_at`, `equilibria(d, box, seeds, seed, distinct)`, classified by the eigenvalues of the Jacobian; `Crossing`, `section(d, x, g, rising, o)`; `Diagram`, `DiagramOptions`, `bifurcation_diagram`; `Basins`, `BasinOptions`, `basins`; `BasinStability`, `basin_stability`, with its standard error √(S(1 − S)/N).
- `criticality.hpp`: `WarningOptions`, `Warnings`, `smooth`, `early_warnings` (variance, autocorrelation at lag 1 and skewness of the residuals in a rolling window, each one's trend by Kendall's τ, against red-noise surrogates); `Fluctuation`, `dfa`; `Avalanches`, `avalanches(activity, threshold)`; `PowerLaw`, `power_law(values, xmin, discrete)` by maximum likelihood, with its standard error and Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance; `Branching`, `branching_ratio(activity, lags)` from the decay of the slopes r_k = b mᵏ.
- `landscape.hpp`: `Potential`, `potential(d, lower, upper, cells)` (U = −∫ f dx of a flow of one dimension), `asymmetry` (whether a flow is a gradient, tested at points), `potential_between`; `stationary_landscape` (exact for one dimension and one source); `kramers_time(gradient, well, barrier, sigma)`; `DensityLandscape`, `DensityOptions`, `density_landscape` (two components, from a long path).
- `operators.hpp`: `Transfer{centres, matrix, stationary, eigenvalues, lost}`, `transfer(d, lower, upper, cells, points, time, component, base)`, Ulam's matrix of one component of a map's iteration or of a flow's map over a time.

*Caveat.* `lyapunov` is for systems without events. Its errors take blocks of the run as independent; where they are not, the error is understated. `equilibria` finds every root its seeds converged to: an equilibrium no seed reached is not found, and the answer does not say there is none. The labels of `basins` are of where paths end at the final time, so an attractor that is not a point may be split.

*Caveat.* A rising indicator is not a transition: trends arise by chance in correlated series, a window chosen after looking inflates them, and transitions driven by noise or by the rate of change of a parameter need give no warning at all. A power law over a decade or two does not show criticality. The slope at lag 1 is biased as an estimate of the branching ratio when only a share of the units is observed.

*Caveat.* In more than one dimension the stationary landscape is estimated from a path as the logarithm of a histogram; it is not a Lyapunov function of the deterministic part in general, and is twice the Freidlin–Wentzell quasi-potential over σ² only in the limit of small noise within a basin. Kramers's formula is for one dimension with additive noise, in the limit ΔU ≫ σ²/2. Ulam's matrix adds a diffusion of the size of a cell, so that only eigenvalues well inside the unit disc of a map with a spectral gap are meaningful.
- `statistics.hpp`: `BlockMean`, `block_mean(series, blocks)`, the mean of a series with the standard error of its block means; `Statistic{name, mean, error, second}`, `StatisticsOptions`, `Statistics`, `averages(d, x, o)`, the means of a system's declared observations along one long path, each with its error and its second moment over its mean squared; `Scaling`, `scaling(x, y)`, the power law through points by least squares on their logarithms, with the error of its exponent.

*Caveat.* The error of a mean is right when a block is long against what the observation remembers, and no test of that is made; the error of an exponent is of the line fitted, over the octaves fitted.

## 3. Checks

68: Lorenz's exponents summing to −(σ + 1 + β), the largest 0.9056 and Kaplan and Yorke's dimension 2.062 within the error of a finite time (Sprott 2003, Table A.1); Hénon's map summing to ln 0.3 exactly, the largest 0.4192; the logistic map at r = 4, ln 2; Lorenz's equilibria, the origin and C±, all saddles at ρ = 28, the pair stable foci at ρ = 20 and losing stability between ρ = 24.7 and 24.8 (the Hopf point 24.7368); a section of the harmonic oscillator crossed every 2π/ω; the logistic map's 1, 2 and 4 points at r = 2.8, 3.2 and 3.5, and van der Pol's maxima of 2.0086 at μ = 1; the basins of x' = x − x³, y' = −y, each half of a symmetric grid, and basin stability ½ within its standard error; an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with variance σ²/(2λ) and lag-1 autocorrelation e^{−λΔt}; variance and autocorrelation trending upward, significantly, where the rate falls towards zero, and not for a stationary process; DFA exponents ½ for white noise and 3/2 for a random walk; a power law sampled by inversion recovered within three standard errors; a branching process seen through a subsample, with m from the decay of the slopes; avalanches of the critical Galton–Watson process with an exponent near 3/2; U = x⁴/4 − x²/2 at every grid point, with its wells and barrier; a gradient flow of two dimensions recognised, and Lorenz not one; the exact stationary landscape 2U/σ² up to a constant, the landscape from a long path agreeing with it, and Kramers's time against the mean of simulated escapes; −ln p = |x|²/σ² for a linear stochastic system with rotation; Ulam's matrix of the logistic map at r = 4 with the density 1/(π√(x(1 − x))), and of the doubling map on a Markov partition of 2⁶ cells with the uniform density reached exactly in six steps.

## 4. Dependencies

`kinesis::systems`, and through it physis's `dynamics` (`flows.hpp`: the Lyapunov spectrum and the Runge–Kutta step); calx's `linalg` (dense matrices, eigenvalues) and `random`; tyche's `descriptive` (moments, Kendall's τ) and `time_series` (autocorrelation, red-noise surrogates). `criticality.hpp` uses `kinesis::kernel` alone among the modules of kinesis. The checks use `kinesis::language` and calx's `verification`.
