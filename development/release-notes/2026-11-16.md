# 2026-11-16

## Balance Laws on Graphs, Learned from a Few Snapshots

kinesis can now learn the laws of a field of thousands of cells from a dozen snapshots: a city's people every fifth year, a concentration over a network ([decision 0005](../decisions/0005-balance-laws-on-graphs.md)). The infrastructure uses it for the Urban Studio's population dynamics ([ADR 0124](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/Noetarium-The-Science-and-Humanity-Infrastructure/blob/main/development/decisions/0124-population-dynamics.md)).

- **A law of a fluid on a graph.** Diffusion, crowding (the porous-medium flux) and drift up a potential, each conservative by construction, with growth (exponential, logistic, Gompertz's), on any graph of cells; a square grid with a coast is the first. A law runs by Runge–Kutta within its stability, and says how much it had to clip.
- **Learned from a few snapshots.** First by the integral form, needing no derivative of the record; then by shooting, running the law and fitting it to every snapshot. A diffusion that comes out negative is ill-posed, and is set aside, said so.
- **Judged honestly.** On snapshots kept aside, against carrying the last one forward, as a whole (how much) and as a pattern (where, once the total is set right).
- **Modes from a few snapshots.** Exact DMD by the method of snapshots, which works with a dozen snapshots of thousands of cells.
- **Checks.** Fisher's front runs at 2√(rD); a law is recovered from five snapshots where the integral form alone is biased; Fisher's three terms are found among eight; the truth lies within the standard errors under noise; a linear field's modes are recovered to rounding. 31 checks, 464 in all.
