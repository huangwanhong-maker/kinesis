# Transitions

Status: **draft** (2026-11-19). Module `kinesis::transitions` ([decision 0008](../development/decisions/0008-critical-transitions-in-structured-states.md)).

## 1. Concepts

A **critical transition** is a change of a system's state that its record may or may not foretell. Near a local bifurcation of codimension one the rate at which perturbations decay goes to zero, and the record's fluctuations grow and lengthen: *critical slowing down*. That is a theorem only for such bifurcations with a clear separation of time scales. A transition driven by noise at fixed parameters, by the rate of change of a parameter, through a global bifurcation, a crisis or a threshold gives no such warning. So every detector here says what it can see and what it is blind to before it looks at a record, and every answer is a verdict with its reasons: **signal**, **no signal** (said only where the test would have seen a slowing of a declared size at the record's length), or **undecidable**. The module rests on the infrastructure's survey *Critical transitions in structured systems* (2026); theory: [docs/theory.md](../docs/theory.md) §6.1.

A state need not be a number. It may be a field over units, a composition (a population by age, an economy by sector), a distribution given by a histogram, or a covariance matrix. Fluctuations are measured in linear coordinates with an inner product, so a **state space** declares a chart: the identity; isometric log-ratios for compositions, with the log total as a further coordinate when asked (what changes the size kept apart from what changes the shape); the quantile function for histograms, whose Euclidean distance approximates Wasserstein's W₂; and the whitened matrix logarithm for covariances, an isometry of the affine-invariant metric at its reference. The units may carry a **neighbourhood** (for spatial indicators) and a **hierarchy** (kinesis::scales'), on which every level is read in two channels: the totals of each group, which keep the mode uniform within it, and the contrasts within each group, which take that mode away.

Every multivariate indicator estimates one of three objects of the linearised system: the spectrum of the lag-1 propagator Â = Ĉ(1)Ĉ(0)⁻¹, the leading eigenpair of the covariance, or a projection on the critical direction. Near a fold, C ≈ (wᵀBBᵀw / 2|λ₁|) v vᵀ: the left eigenvector w says whether the noise excites the critical mode and the right eigenvector v where it shows. A mode the noise does not excite shows no rise in variance; a mode uniform across units shows in their mean and not in their spread. The assessment reports v as a field over the units (where), and w (what excites it).

A trend in an indicator is tested by the **change** between the first and the last window (a log ratio for positive indicators), against records of the stationary linear process fitted to the residuals; the same pipeline run on that null and on its **declared alternative** (the leading mode's rate falling to a tenth over the record, the noise unchanged) gives the test's false-alarm rate, detection rate and ROC at the record's own length. Kendall's τ is reported as the literature reports it, but it measures monotonicity and not size, and with windows of half the record it is near ±1 by chance too often to have power. Other nulls (random phases, IAAFT, the block bootstrap) and a grid of windows and bandwidths say whether a significance survives the choices that made it.

*Caveat.* The verdicts rest on the fitted linear null: a record whose fluctuations are far from linear and Gaussian is judged against a null it does not resemble, which the further nulls only partly correct. The declared alternative is one size of slowing; the detection rate is against it and no other. The red-noise rate trades variance for robustness: its window must span many correlation times, and its interval is carried over from the AR(1) estimate. Detrending with a bandwidth comparable to the record's correlation time near the transition removes part of the slowing it would show, biasing every rate upward there. Rate-induced tipping is detectable only with a model of the forcing; nothing here claims it from a record.

## 3. Checks

77 (`tests/check_transitions.cpp`): the four charts against closed forms (Aitchison's distance, a normal histogram's quantiles, the affine-invariant distance) and their refusals; the Lyapunov equations at their solutions; a non-normal VAR(1)'s eigenvalues and both eigenvectors recovered, its covariance at the Lyapunov solution; λ_max(C₀⁻¹C) invariant under a linear change of coordinates; shrinkage conditioning a short window; an OU process's κ by three estimators and its variance σ²/(2κ); the red-noise autocorrelation symmetric, continuous and matched by simulation, its rates recovered where the AR(1) estimate confounds them, and holding steady while noise reddens; Kendall's τ's variance 2(2n + 5)/(9n(n − 1)); surrogates keeping the periodogram, the values and the observed values; a fold detected with its eigenvalue going to +1; false alarms of a stationary OU process at the stated level; rate-induced tipping at constant r showing no falling rate while r > 1 tips; a double well read as two wells with switches; a period-doubling with autocorrelation falling and the eigenvalue going to −1; the Hopf normal form's eigenvalue at e^{μΔt} and ωΔt, its slowing detected and pointing to the circle in two coordinates and in one; a drifting rate preferred by likelihood with the truth inside its tipping interval and none given for a constant rate; on a network the uniform mode found and spatial variance changing less than the covariance, one unit's slowing located there and detected by the relative growth; a mode the noise does not excite giving no signal; a composition's log total carrying a slowing its shares do not; a hierarchy's totals carrying a slowing its shares within do not; an irregular and a short record refused.
